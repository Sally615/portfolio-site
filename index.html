02 — Projects

Selected work

01

Experiment platform · Governance

Catching harmful experiments before they cost real money

The problem. In e-commerce, every experiment touches revenue. With hundreds of tests live at once, a losing variant could keep running at high traffic long before anyone stepped in, and the platform had no way to stop it.

  • Set tiered alerts on GMV per user: a significant drop pages the experiment owner, a severe one goes straight to leadership.
  • Added a ramp-up gate, so a test only moves from small to full traffic when core metrics show no harm.
  • Introduced Launch Review, so no feature ships by trading away core metrics or another team’s module.

Started in the U.S., then rolled out to Europe and Southeast Asia as the standard for TikTok Global E-Commerce.

200+harmful experiments stopped each quarter
$10M+GMV loss avoided each quarter
30+platform features designed and launched
02

Product analytics · Machine learning

Finding what actually makes shoppers buy

The problem. The product detail page is the front door of the purchase funnel, yet each of its modules was judged by its own isolated A/B test. Nobody had a view of the whole page.

  • Trained a CatBoost model on product attributes to rank what drives conversion from page view to order.
  • Distilled the result into a three-tier framework: promotions, sales volume, and price lead; shipping, benefits, and reviews follow; delivery time and content depth come third.
  • Turned the findings into a page-structure roadmap with product and design, and into a listing-quality score that guides sellers.

Methods: CatBoost classification, feature importance, funnel and conversion analysis.

20+feature launches shipped from the roadmap, with measurable click-to-order gains
3 tiersof conversion drivers, now reused in page redesigns
03

Applied AI · LLM tooling

From experiment results to shared knowledge

The problem. Summarizing dozens of concurrent experiments took days, and much of the team’s know-how lived only in people’s heads.

  • Built an LLM pipeline on the Claude API that synthesizes results across experiments into one readable summary. It spread well beyond my own team.
  • Prototyped a team knowledge base: structured, cross-linked Markdown notes in Obsidian that an LLM organizes and reasons over, covering methodology, past analyses, and institutional memory.

When a process doesn’t scale, I build the infrastructure to fix it. LLMs are the newest tool for that.

Days → hoursdata science reporting time
04

Experiment design · Holdouts

Measuring what thirty launches really add up to

The problem. The team shipped dozens of features a quarter, each tested alone. But lifts don’t simply add up: some features compound, others cancel each other out.

  • Proposed the first container (holdout) experiments on the user product side and co-designed “Domains” with the platform team, so a clean holdout group can coexist with every ongoing A/B test.
  • Created a seed-finder to keep small holdout samples balanced, and stacked reversal parameters so the holdout mirrors each new launch.
  • Brought the winner’s-curse correction from Airbnb’s KDD 2018 paper into the platform as a standard feature.

Now the standard way data science teams across TikTok Shop measure combined feature impact.

Firstholdout framework on the user product side
300+data scientists in the session where I presented it
05

Causal inference · Personalization

One page doesn’t fit every shopper

The problem. Every shopper saw the same product page, whether they wandered in from a video or searched with a clear goal.

  • Used causal forests to estimate how each module’s effect varies by how a shopper arrived.
  • Found that feed shoppers respond to social proof and creator content, while search shoppers want price, specs, and shipping.
  • The finding led to “See It in Action,” a creator video module on the product page.

Methods: heterogeneous treatment effects, Generalized Random Forests.

+1.3%GMV, statistically significant

All projects are from my time at TikTok Global E-Commerce, 2023–2026.
